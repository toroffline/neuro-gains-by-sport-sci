# Evidence and editorial notes

This four-minute educational adaptation preserves the supplied six-scene structure and timing. The original unsupported percentage ranges and absolute clinical claims were replaced with contextual explanations. Models are shaded, schematic 3D meshes, not anatomically validated reconstructions or quantitative biological simulations. Particle counts, gland sizes, fluid movement, and bone changes are illustrative. Bone adaptation occurs over months; animation compresses time. English narration uses Windows Microsoft Zira speech synthesis.

## Changes to the supplied brief

- The free tryptophan / competing amino acid mechanism is presented as a central-fatigue hypothesis, not an established single cause. No universal aerobic 200–400% or resistance 10–30% ratio change is asserted. Resistance exercise can also cause central fatigue. [Meeusen et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-200636100-00006).
- BCAAs are not described as a proven central-fatigue blocker. [Review of nutritional considerations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10919962/) and [brain amino acid review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18577773/).
- Hemoconcentration can change measured progesterone concentration, but it does not prove the absence of secretion changes or exclude the corpus luteum. Acute ranges and recovery times in the brief were omitted because they were not linked to a population, cycle phase, or experimental protocol. [Menstrual cycle and exercise metabolism](https://doi.org/10.2165/11317090-000000000-00000).
- Receptor-specific training adaptations are not asserted as universal in humans. Much mechanistic evidence is preclinical. The supplied Greenwood and Fleshner citation had the wrong journal; corrected to *Exercise and Sport Sciences Reviews*, 39(3), 140–149. [Article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303035/).
- Low energy availability can affect any training modality. No universal 50–80% progesterone reduction, guaranteed rapid bone loss, or resistance-mediated hormonal protection is claimed. Bone health reflects multiple factors, including estrogen and nutrition, not progesterone alone. [IOC REDs consensus, 2023](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073).
- A carbohydrate range of 30–60 g/h is contextual guidance for sustained exercise, not a guarantee of preventing central fatigue. [ISSN nutrient timing position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28919842/).
- A single progesterone result above 5 ng/mL is not a universal health target. Timing depends on the individual's cycle; single measurements cannot determine luteal-phase quality. [ASRM fertility evaluation guidance](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/).
- No single energy availability threshold is prescribed to every athlete. Clinical assessment and restoration of adequate energy availability should be individualized. [IOC consensus](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073).

The film includes selected references, with additional clinical interpretation sources here. It omits the general textbook and historical Newsholme citation from the on-screen list to preserve legibility. The references remain stationary for more than ten seconds, followed by a fade to white and final black frame.

## Movement and Thai revision

Fixed-segment rigs replace the original joint paths. The runner uses two-link inverse kinematics and a belt matched to stance-foot velocity. Curl shoulders and elbow roots remain stationary. This illustrative motion is not measured motion capture or a universal technique prescription. See [running biomechanics analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4714754/) and [ACE biceps guidance](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/9033/building-better-biceps-evidence-based-exercises-for-strength-and-function/). Thai localization includes text, controls, captions, and synthesized Microsoft Niwat Neural narration. References retain their original publication language.


## Object identification and anatomical schematics (v4)

Every explanatory scene now has numbered labels and a synchronized bilingual object guide. The vessel is a longitudinal cutaway; coral discs denote red blood cells. Gold dots denote free tryptophan in the transport scene and progesterone in the concentration scene; gold surface cells in the bone scene denote osteoblasts. These are context-specific symbols, not a universal color code.

Raphe markers were relocated to the brainstem below the cerebral mass, consistent with [human raphe anatomy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14729135/). The HPO diagram instead highlights the hypothalamic region and a separate pituitary gland. The adrenal is drawn above a bean-shaped kidney as a location landmark; see [OpenStax adrenal anatomy](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/17-6-the-adrenal-glands). The long-bone cutaway distinguishes an outer shell and an internal trabecular lattice; see [OpenStax bone structure](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-3-bone-structure).

The glucose model summarizes connectivity; hydrogen atoms and stereochemistry are omitted. A capped blood tube represents sampling rather than an assay result. Models are illustrative procedural meshes with simplified surfaces and relative dimensions. They are not anatomical scans, chemically validated conformations, quantitative concentration simulations, or patient-specific reconstructions. Existing narration and scene timings are retained.
