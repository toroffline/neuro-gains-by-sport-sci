# What the image represents: object guide

Numbers match the film and website labels. Shapes, scale, particle counts, and compressed timing are teaching schematics rather than patient-specific anatomy or measurements.

## 1. Whole athlete

### 1. Brain

Coral marker in the head identifies the neural component.

### 2. Heart

Chest marker identifies the heart; its position is schematic.

### 3. Adrenal / ovarian sites

Abdominal and pelvic markers locate endocrine organs; enlarged views follow.

### 4. Runner and lifter

The runner represents aerobic activity; the lifter represents resistance exercise.

## 2. Blood, brain and serotonin

### 1. Albumin

Blue lobed proteins carry bound tryptophan in plasma.

### 2. Free fatty acids (FFA)

Orange bead chains represent fatty acids competing at albumin binding sites.

### 3. Blood vessel / cutaway

Pink walls surround the lumen. Coral discs are red blood cells, not hormones.

### 4. Muscle fibers and BCAA

Striped parallel fibers represent muscle. Blue amino-acid markers move toward it.

### 5. BBB and LAT1

An endothelial barrier and blue transporter represent selective amino-acid entry. Gold dots are free tryptophan.

### 6. Brainstem raphe nuclei

Coral points lie in the brainstem below the cerebral mass; they represent serotonergic nuclei.

## 3. Acute P4 interpretation

### 1. Plasma compartment

The vessel cross-section shows a smaller fluid compartment with the same illustrative particle count.

### 2. P4 concentration markers

Gold dots represent progesterone; closer spacing illustrates concentration, not a measured secretion rate.

### 3. Fluid movement

Blue droplets outside the vessel indicate a fluid shift.

### 4. Adrenal gland above a kidney

The golden cap is the adrenal gland. The bean-shaped kidney below is a location landmark.

### 5. Ovary / corpus luteum

Follicles and a golden corpus luteum appear inside an ovary cutaway. Ovarian contribution depends on cycle phase.

## 4. Chronic adaptation

### 1. Hypothalamus

The underside brain marker represents the hypothalamic region, not the entire brain.

### 2. Pituitary gland

A small two-lobed gland connects to the brain by a stalk; the arrow summarizes signaling toward the ovary.

### 3. Ovary and energy gauge

The ovary completes the HPO schematic. The gauge is an illustrative energy-availability indicator, not a test result.

### 4. Long bone / cutaway

A cortical shell and an internal trabecular lattice make the bone structure visible.

### 5. Osteoblasts and loading

Gold surface cells represent osteoblasts. The dumbbell and arrow indicate mechanical loading over time.

## 5. Application

### 1. Glucose / molecular schematic

A ring-and-stick model indicates glucose connectivity. Blue is carbon, coral is oxygen; hydrogens and stereochemistry are omitted.

### 2. Blood collection tube

The capped cylinder contains a blood sample. It represents sampling, not a simulated laboratory readout.

### 3. Cycle-aware P4 assessment

The timing marker indicates sampling in relation to the individual menstrual cycle.
